For over fifteen years, I was the person engineers, support, and solutions consultants turned to when they needed to know how a feature actually worked — because I was usually the one who'd figured it out and written it down in the first place. My title said Product Owner or Solutions Architect, but the actual work was documentation: specs an engineering team could build from, requirements support could rely on, release notes that told a customer exactly what changed and why.

I never experienced writing and design as two separate jobs. Deciding how a feature should behave and writing the spec that describes it were the same act of thinking — the writing was where the design was initially tested. That's still how I work, and it's why the case studies in this portfolio aren't just polished prose: they're a record of decisions getting made in real time, not documentation written after the fact.

I'm now formalizing that experience into technical writing specifically — building out this portfolio, picking up industry-standard tools like Confluence and Markdown-based docs-as-code, and looking for a role where clear writing is the job itself, not just a skill I happened to use along the way.

Based in Palmdale, CA. Open to full-time, part-time, or contract work — remote-friendly.

Cimply — a two-sided scheduling tool

Cimply is a fictional work-order and scheduling tool for small creative teams, built specifically to give this portfolio a real product to document. It has two user-facing dashboards below. There's no separate Admin dashboard — that's a deliberate product decision, not an omission: Admin is a permission flag layered onto the Scheduler role rather than its own persona, so it doesn't get its own screen.
